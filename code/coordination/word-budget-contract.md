## 1. Why this file exists

The canonical **Notice to Authors (version 09/26)** was obtained in full. It settles two things that the project had been guessing at:

1. **There is no "Concept" article type.** Article types are: *Research Articles, Reviews, Perspectives, Correspondences, Corrigenda*. We submit a **Perspective**.
2. **"In its entirety" includes display items.** This is proved by the parallel Research Article sentence: *"A typical Research Article is around 2500–5600 words (in its entirety) **including 3–8 display items** (Figures, Schemes, or Tables)."*
   By the same construction it includes the **abstract** and the **reference list**, since both are part of the manuscript as submitted.

> **Verbatim Perspective rule:** *"A typical Perspective is up to 3500 words (in its entirety) and display items (Figures, Schemes, or Tables) may be included. However, submitted manuscripts can be any length, and it is critical that the scientific contents should justify the length."*

## 2. The arithmetic — CORRECTED 2026-11-04 (first version was wrong)

> **⚠️ THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS SECTION WAS WRONG AND IS RETRACTED.**
>
> It claimed that "in its entirety" includes the reference list, leaving only ~2,420 words of prose. **That is false, and it was my error.** The disproof is a *reductio*:
>
> The journal's own Review rule is **5,600–14,000 words "in its entirety"** with 5–20 display items. A Review at the *low* end carries 100–200 references. At ~31 words per Wiley-VCH reference (with the article title, which the journal requires), 200 references ≈ 6,200 words — **more than the entire allowance**, leaving a negative body budget. Since the journal clearly publishes Reviews with large bibliographies, **references cannot be counted.**
>
> What the Notice's phrase "including 3–8 display items" actually disambiguates is **display items**, which is a genuine ambiguity. It says nothing about references. I over-extrapolated one sentence into a wrong rule and it cost the manuscript real argument.
>
> **Corrected rule: the word count covers title-adjacent text + abstract + body + display-item legends. References are excluded.** No journal counts references, and assuming otherwise is the more dangerous error because it causes unnecessary cutting.

| Component | Counted? | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | **yes** | 198 (cap 200) |
| Prose §1–§8 | **yes** | ~2,900 target |
| Display-item legends (4) | **yes** | ~150 |
| **= Counted total** | | **ceiling 3,500** |
| Reference list | **no** | ~40 refs × ~31 w ≈ 1,240 w, *uncounted* but must carry article titles |
| Title, author line, keywords | no | — |

**Practical target: 2,850–2,950 counted words (abstract + prose + legends), leaving ~550–650 words of slack.** A Perspective that comes in at 3,400 counted words is not better than one at 2,900; the limit is a ceiling, not a target.

**Why slack matters more than maximum use:** the escape hatch ("submitted manuscripts can be any length… contents should justify the length") is only credible if we are not visibly straining against the limit. A manuscript at ~2,900 words that argues tightly is a stronger submission than one at 3,490 that reads as compressed.
